abstract: "As befits a book about language learning, Phyllis Gaffney has a way with\
  \ words, and she has achieved an erudite, entertaining and enlightening read. Indeed,\
  \ the very title points to the significance language learning in Victorian Ireland\
  \ held in the creation of modern Ireland. But to view this book solely as a treatise\
  \ on this subject is to pigeon-hole this illuminating tome. Gaffney scrutinises\
  \ language learning by Irish scholars, and analyses themes as diverse as empire,\
  \ philology and the Counter-Reformation, as well as more familiar themes like class,\
  \ politics and gender. The kernel of Phyllis Gaffney\u2019s book is that Ireland\u2019\
  s engagement with learning languages ultimately led to languages being taught equally\
  \ to male and female students. Throughout, there are countless stories of men and\
  \ women whose lives were changed by learning, teaching and working with modern languages,\
  \ and she illustrates how such learning has had consequences for Irish people\u2019\
  s sense of identity. To do so, her research was meticulous, extending to 700 primary\
  \ and secondary sources, and she has judiciously used personal letters to add to\
  \ the sense of fulfilment expressed by students of languages."

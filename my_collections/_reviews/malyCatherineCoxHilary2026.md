abstract: "The topic of mental health in institutions has received significant study\
  \ among historians, with a focus on asylums and workhouses. In this work, Cox and\
  \ Marland contribute to understandings of mental breakdown in prisons. They aim\
  \ to investigate the prison system and the accusations that it produced and exacerbated\
  \ mental illness. It analyses the punitive regimes that destroyed the minds of prisoners\
  \ and how they faced obstruction to treatment. This work joins a very limited literature\
  \ on mental breakdowns in prisons, with the cross-comparison between England and\
  \ Ireland being particularly unique. The comparative analysis allows exploration\
  \ of the differences between England and Ireland. In the era presented in this book,\
  \ England was a colonial power over Ireland. Thus, any differences identified may\
  \ be indicative of colonial racial attitudes. The primary contribution of Cox and\
  \ Marland\u2019s scholarship lies in their documentation of the consistently high\
  \ prevalence of mental illness within English and Irish penal institutions. Their\
  \ analysis exposes a systemic refusal among prison officials to accept accountability\
  \ for inmate insanity, noting a tendency to attribute pre-existing psychiatric issues\
  \ such as mania and melancholy."

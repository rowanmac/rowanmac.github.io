abstract: "Anything Henry Crawford says should be taken with a pinch of salt. He is,\
  \ after all, one of the many Jane Austen creations who delights in hyperbole and\
  \ winking satire. Nevertheless, his take on Shakespeare recorded in Mansfield Park\
  \ is often quoted without irony: \u201CShakespeare one gets acquainted with without\
  \ knowing how. It is part of an Englishman\u2019s constitution. His thoughts and\
  \ beauties are so spread abroad that one touches them everywhere; one is intimate\
  \ with him by instinct.\u201D In recent decades, Global Shakespeare studies has\
  \ focused on the tension hidden in Crawford\u2019s arch statement. From the eighteenth\
  \ century onward, Shakespeare\u2019s works represented a quintessential Englishness\
  \ while being \u201Cspread abroad.\u201D At the same time, Shakespeare became a\
  \ symbol of Englishness beyond the plays themselves. Without needing to have read\
  \ or seen a performance of a Shakespeare play, one could come into a kind of osmotic\
  \ contact with Shakespeare all over the world. The contributions to Shakespeare\
  \ in Ireland: Adaptations and Appropriations, expertly edited by Andrew Murphy,\
  \ argue that Shakespeare not only was an English cultural icon, but also a crucial,\
  \ contested symbol in Ireland from the eighteenth to the twenty-first century\u2014\
  one that operated differently from its English counterpart."

abstract: ", Good local history can provide a better key to understanding the past\
  \ and the complexity of relations on our small island than broader national surveys,\
  \ which often fail to capture the reality of our ancestors\u2019 lives., This book\
  \ by Fergus O\u2019Farrell is an example of local history at its best. [End Page\
  \ 512] The author, as a trained historian, combines a scholarly approach to his\
  \ subject with a deep understanding of the people and the geography of his native\
  \ county., In the prologue, he says that the Irish local historian is required to\
  \ examine \u2018the tensions, conflicts, disruptions, and accommodations that arose\
  \ between \u201Cnewcomers\u201D and \u201Cnatives\u201D particularly in relation\
  \ to land ownership and use but also in respect of language, culture, trade and\
  \ religion.\u2019, He suggests that Irish local historians must operate within two\
  \ broad cultures, the Gaelic and Anglicised way of seeing the world and their different\
  \ perceptions of local territories. Only by doing that will they be able to understand\
  \ people within the landscape in which they have lived and even helped to shape.,\
  \ The author argues that local history matters, not just as another enjoyable pastime\
  \ or an academic discipline, but as a form of knowledge that promotes healthy cultural\
  \ development as well as informing current social and political challenges. He says\
  \ that local historians, if they are true to their task, will establish a close\
  \ and critical rapport with all the sources of a community\u2019s past, not shrinking\
  \ from the pain and trauma of past conflicts but recounting the stories of past\
  \ generations with empathy and sensitivity for all., He quotes the great philosopher\
  \ Spinoza: \u2018I have striven not to laugh at human actions, not to weep at them,\
  \ nor to hate them but to understand them.\u2019, The early chapters of the book\
  \ trace the evolution of \u2018O\u2019Ferrall countrie\u2019 from around AD 1100\
  \ up to the end of the Gaelic world and the emergence of a new ruling class in the\
  \ aftermath of the Cromwellian plantation. For centuries after the Norman invasion,\
  \ the O\u2019Ferrall\u2019s managed to hold on their territory through a mixture\
  \ of resistance and guile., Within the territory there was a marked difference between\
  \ the approach of the O\u2019Ferrall B\xE1n in the north of the county, who resisted\
  \ the encroachments of the Dublin administration, and the Farrell Bu\xED to the\
  \ south of them, who survived by adaption and accommodation., The O\u2019Ferrall\
  \ B\xE1n rallied to Hugh O\u2019Neill, while their southern neighbours tried to\
  \ stay out of the conflict and later used the courts in Dublin and London to protect\
  \ their interests. In south Longford, English was widely spoken long before it spread\
  \ to the north of the county., The leading Ferralls managed to hold on to most of\
  \ their land in the Plantation of Longford in 1619, but it was a short-lived reprieve.\
  \ The Cromwellian settlement, followed by the Williamite one, transformed land [End\
  \ Page 513] ownership in the county and entrenched a new, mainly Protestant land\
  \ owning class at the apex of society. Some of these families, like the Edgeworths,\
  \ made important contributions to society both locally and nationally., This ascendency\
  \ was challenged in the nineteenth century by the emergence of democratic politics\
  \ in the wake of Catholic Emancipation. The author, who completed a pioneering work\
  \ on the development of the O\u2019Connellite political organisation in Longford\
  \ for his doctorate, expands on the theme in the book and traces the evolution of\
  \ politics in the county up to the time of the crucial South Longford by election\
  \ of 1917., Elections in Longford in the 1830s were bitter, sectarian affairs, which\
  \ stirred up tensions between the Catholic and Protestant communities. The weight\
  \ of numbers ultimately saw to it that the Catholic/nationalist side prevailed in\

  \ impact on the ownership of land., O\u2019Ferrall\u2019s focus is wider than politics.\

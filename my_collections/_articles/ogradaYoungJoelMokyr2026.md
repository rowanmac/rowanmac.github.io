abstract: Joel Mokyr was awarded the Nobel Prize in Economics in December 2025 for
  his insights into the links between institutions, technological change and economic
  progress. The research that underpinned his findings contrasts strongly with his
  early career work. The young Mokyr was, above all, a deductive scholar; his forte
  was in using abstract models in order to test hypotheses about economic development
  and underdevelopment. The scholarship of the older Mokyr, by contrast, is highly
  inductive in approach. Nonetheless, the lessons learned early in his career form
  part of the accumulated knowledge that fed his later work, and deserve to be better
  known. This paper offers a critical summary of Mokyr's early research, dating mainly
  from the 1970s and 1980s, which is interesting and important in its own right.

abstract: "This article reads Mary St. John\u2019s 1815 verse narrative \u201CEllauna:\
  \ A Legend of the Thirteenth Century\u201D to examine the troubled process of reclaiming\
  \ Ireland\u2019s medieval past in early nineteenth-century Ireland. Situating Ellauna\
  \ within the tradition of the Irish national tale, the article foregrounds how verse\
  \ and paratext work in concert to complicate resolution and settlement in post-Union\
  \ Ireland. It argues that Ellauna enacts a form of topographical haunting whereby\
  \ the Irish landscape functions both as a site of commemoration and of unresolved\
  \ historical tension and emerges as a layered site of memory where personal, regional,\
  \ national, and global histories converge. By reading the physical environment itself\
  \ as a medium of cultural persistence and spectral unsettlement, this article seeks\
  \ to expand the purview of the Irish national tale and to reconsider women\u2019\
  s contributions to the curation of local history in the early nineteenth century."

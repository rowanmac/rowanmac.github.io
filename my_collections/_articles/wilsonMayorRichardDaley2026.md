abstract: "Between September 1969 and December 1972, Mayor Richard J. Daley of Chicago\
  \ sent almost a quarter of a million dollars to charitable organisations that were\
  \ helping victims of violence in Northern Ireland. He also issued scathing attacks\
  \ on British and unionist government policies, particularly over the introduction\
  \ of internment and the murder of fourteen civil rights demonstrators on Bloody\
  \ Sunday. In addition, from 1973 onwards, Daley worked closely with the Dublin government\
  \ to encourage Chicago\u2019s Irish community to support peaceful means to end the\
  \ conflict in Northern Ireland.Why Daley, arguably the most powerful urban leader\
  \ in the United States during these years, with both statewide and national political\
  \ clout, chose to devote his time and energy to the Northern Ireland conflict is\
  \ the focus of this article. Drawing primarily on the mayor\u2019s personal papers,\
  \ it highlights the multiple factors that drove his activism, considers how these\
  \ forces changed over time and assesses the impact of his initiatives both in Northern\
  \ Ireland and in Chicago. While Daley\u2019s financial disbursements and criticisms\
  \ of the Westminster and Stormont governments had important impacts, this article\
  \ argues that his most significant contribution was assisting a campaign by the\
  \ Irish and US governments that undercut the effectiveness of Chicago\u2019s IRA\
  \ support network."

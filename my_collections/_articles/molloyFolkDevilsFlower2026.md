abstract: "This article examines the social construction of the hippie counter-culture\
  \ in Ireland during the period 1967\u201372. It outlines three main phases in this\
  \ construction: namely, sensitisation, mobilisation and diversification. International\
  \ developments sensitised the Irish press to align the hippies with drug abuse,\
  \ violence and murder. The emergence of a commune on Dorinish Island, County Mayo\
  \ sparked the mobilisation of interest groups such as the Irish Countrywomen\u2019\
  s Association who attempted to frame the hippies as folk devils. The hippies, however,\
  \ diversified social knowledge by effectively offering resistance to hostile reactions.\
  \ The final section considers the extent to which the counter-culture in Ireland\
  \ may be regarded as distinctive. It argues that the absence of direct mobilisation\
  \ by the Catholic Church and the presence of colonial discourses emerge as noteworthy\
  \ features. Overall, this article makes important empirical contributions to the\
  \ history of the counter-culture by offering new insights on its manifestation in\
  \ an Irish context. It also contributes conceptual insights into how folk devils\
  \ are constructed from various vantage points and rooted in specific historical\
  \ contexts."

abstract: 'This article explores the impact of the 1916 Proclamation of the Irish
  Republic in Bengal. In pre-partitioned Bengal (before 1947), revolutionary activities
  were strongly influenced by events in Ireland. This article discusses how Irish
  influence is evident in two key Bengali examples: the proclamation issued by Surya
  Sen of the Indian Republican Army (IRA) in 1930 and the proclamation issued by Subhas
  Chandra Bose of the Indian National Army (INA) in 1943. By dissecting the three
  proclamations, the article discusses how the 1916 proclamation offered a blueprint
  for Bengali revolutionaries, while also highlighting the uniqueness of the Bengali
  proclamations which differed fundamentally from the Gandhian approach to the Indian
  liberation movement. The article also provides an overview of lesser known texts
  produced in Bengali relating to the Irish revolution and discusses how the English
  language became a shared language of connection and solidarity among colonized peoples.'

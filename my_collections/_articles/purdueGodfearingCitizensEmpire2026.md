abstract: "This article examines the emigration of children from Belfast workhouse\
  \ in the opening years of the twentieth century. Belfast, a thriving industrial\
  \ city at the heart of empire and itself a product of colonial expansion, was the\
  \ only Irish poor union to emigrate children during this period. The article explores\
  \ the ideas of child welfare, the commitment to empire, and the shared identity\
  \ with Canada\u2019s settler class that encouraged Belfast\u2019s female poor law\
  \ guardians to start emigrating workhouse boys to the empire at a time when the\
  \ Union at the heart of that empire was under threat. It reveals the logistical\
  \ considerations Belfast\u2019s guardians navigated in establishing the system,\
  \ and the anxieties they experienced when the reality of a better start in life\
  \ for these boys did not seem to live up to their expectations. Finally, it seeks\
  \ to recover something of the experience of the emigrated boys themselves asking\
  \ to what extent these Belfast boys did actually become \u2018Empire builders\u2019\
  ."

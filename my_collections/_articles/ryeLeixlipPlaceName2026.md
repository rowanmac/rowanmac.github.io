abstract: "<p>The town of Leixlip takes its name from a salmon leap on the River Liffey.\
  \ For over 170 years the place name has been assumed to be of Scandinavian origin,\
  \ an interpretation that has had considerable influence on historical and archaeo-logical\
  \ studies. The Old Norse (ON) precursor was initially reconstructed as *\\*lax-hlaup*\
  \ \u2018salmon leap\u2019, then refined to *\\*lax-hleypa* \u2018salmon\u2019s leaping-place\u2019\
  . Although the latter form has been widely accepted since 1976, it presents unresolved\
  \ phonological difficulties, while derivation from Middle English has been dismissed\
  \ without detailed analysis. A full reassessment of the evidence, including an examination\
  \ of the relevant English toponymy, suggests that *Leixlip* is better understood\
  \ as a Middle English (ME) compound. If correct, this etymology would significantly\
  \ enhance our understanding of the history of settlement in the area. It would also\
  \ suggest that Ireland\u2019s attested non-urban Scandinavian place names are confined\
  \ entirely to the coast, allowing a more coherent explanation for their survival\
  \ in the English-language stratum of toponymy.</p>"

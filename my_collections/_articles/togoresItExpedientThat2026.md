abstract: "This article compares the two 1923 plays through the plot of\nmartyrdom\
  \ as a discourse of contestation. The audience is faced\nwith a female character\
  \ undergoing a process of martyrdom\u2014Joan of\nArc and Minnie Powell\u2014while\
  \ the playwrights use them and the\nbackgrounds and themes of the plays to discuss\
  \ armed nationalism,\nclass issues, and the active nature of women\u2019s utopian\
  \ beliefs.\nThere are, however, marked differences between the two plays as\nfemininity\
  \ defines the character of Minnie while the lack of it\ndefines that of Joan, and\
  \ as \nThe Shadow of a Gunmanis definitely rooted in an Irish\ncontext while \n\
  Saint Joandistances itself from it to the point where it\nis rarely defined as an\
  \ Irish play. Because, in part, of the\nsimilarities between O\u2019Casey and Shaw\
  \ as much as because of their\n\u201CIrishness,\u201D their staging of the plot\
  \ of martyrdom as a spectacle\nis a way to subvert the hegemonic discourse of the\n\
  nation-in-creation as regards to the place of women in it. Joan and\nMinnie come\
  \ to represent the subalterns of their society and try,\nunsuccessfully, to make\
  \ themselves heard by the ones who could\nchange the established order\u2014the\
  \ audience."

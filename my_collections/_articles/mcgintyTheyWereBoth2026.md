abstract: "This article examines Gaelic succession in early modern Ireland and describes\
  \ its impact on political stability within clans. Referred to as tanistry, Gaelic\
  \ succession had several characteristics that contributed to internal clan succession\
  \ disputes. Tanistry included a designated successor known as the *t\xE1naiste*,\
  \ who was often a prominent member of the clan. This prominence made them a potential\
  \ rival to the ruling chieftain due to their significant influence. Gaelic succession\
  \ also had an elective component and unclear criteria used to determine who should\
  \ succeed. This made multiple claimants to the chieftainship possible, and could\
  \ lead to disagreement and sometimes internecine wars of succession. Succession\
  \ was further complicated by the presence of the English, who attempted to introduce\
  \ primogeniture into Ireland and so, there were two modes of succession in Early\
  \ Modern Ireland. The English also helped exacerbate internal clan violence as they\
  \ played clan rivals off each when it suited them.</p>"

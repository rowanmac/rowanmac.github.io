abstract: Anglicisation in this context is the assimilation of names into the dominant
  English culture, whether for Jewish immigrants or native Gaels. Biblical names were
  present in Ireland via both Christianity and English immigration before the period
  under consideration here, while the process of anglicisation of Gaelic names led
  to the emergence of surnames in Ireland that are typically regarded as Jewish elsewhere,
  at a time when there were effectively no Jews in Ireland. The arrival of 5000 Jews
  in the late nineteenth century, many bearing variants of those names, led to an
  enrichment of the range of names in the country in a political era when names were
  regarded as an important signifier of cultural and political identity. The legacy
  of that enrichment has remained throughout the twentieth century and beyond.

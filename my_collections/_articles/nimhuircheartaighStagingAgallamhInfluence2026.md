abstract: The literary and cultural revival of the early twentieth century in Ireland
  created a new stage for drama and traditional arts practice. Innovation and experimentation
  were also features of the dramatic movement of the time. This article gives an account
  of the recitation and performance of oral poetry at Gaelic League concerts and competitions
  in the revival and focuses on the agallamh beirte, or poetic dispute, in particular.
  It examines the interest dramatists took in the oral tradition and shows the influence
  the agallamh as a literary form had on the dramatic works of Alice Milligan and
  Lady Gregory. Other key figures and influential dramatists of the revival period
  are also discussed in this regard, including W.B. Yeats, Douglas Hyde, Patrick Pearse
  and John Millington Synge.

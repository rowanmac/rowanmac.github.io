abstract: "This article examines how the Northern Ireland trade union movement confronted\
  \ state repression during the Troubles, focusing on the short-lived Trade Union\
  \ Campaign Against Repression (TUCAR) and the death of AUEW-TASS shop steward Brian\
  \ Maguire in Castlereagh RUC barracks in May 1978. It argues that TUCAR\u2019s activism\
  \ exposed a central contradiction within organised labour: leaderships were troubled\
  \ by repression yet remained institutionally bound to the Northern state. Drawing\
  \ on a range of archival sources, press coverage, and trade union records, the article\
  \ reconstructs several campaigns around state repression in Northern Ireland. It\
  \ demonstrates how the political weakness of the labour movement led it to seek\
  \ partnership with the state, limiting union responses to repression, even amid\
  \ mounting evidence of brutality. The failure to mount an independent labour response\
  \ to repression led to the emergence of TUCAR, which was a marginal actor, rooted\
  \ in the nationalist community. This reinforced a flawed, dangerous perception that\
  \ repression was a nationalist issue. The article concludes that trade unions were\
  \ uniquely positioned to articulate a class-based critique of state violence but\
  \ ultimately failed to do so."

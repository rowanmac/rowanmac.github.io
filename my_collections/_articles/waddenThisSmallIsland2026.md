abstract: To date, relatively little attention has been paid to immigration as a feature
  of early medieval Irish society, or to the experience of immigrants in early medieval
  Ireland. This is in spite of the fact that immigrants and immigration are both well
  attested across a variety of extant sources from the period, including histories
  and chronicles, legal texts, sagas, hagiography, and the archaeological record.
  This article surveys these diverse sources to highlight the scope and significance
  of immigration in early Ireland. Within the limits imposed by the sources, it examines
  the different kinds of immigrants who came to Ireland in the early period in terms
  of their social status (before and after their migration), gender, and ethnicity,
  as well as the motives that brought them to Ireland, their experiences as immigrants,
  and local attitudes toward them. It demonstrates the considerable diversity that
  characterised both the immigrants themselves and their experiences.

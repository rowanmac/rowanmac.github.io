abstract: "Ceremonies of churching tend to have a negative reputation, as rituals\
  \ imposed by clerics on women in the vulnerable time after childbirth. Their perceived\
  \ associations with \u2018cleansing\u2019 or \u2018purification\u2019 have long\
  \ caused disquiet. However, the term might be applied to quite different rites,\
  \ there were different views of churching\u2019s nature and purpose, and it was\
  \ often promoted by people other than the clergy. The sources relating to churching\
  \ are unusually difficult: neither the Catholic Church nor the Church of Ireland\
  \ were inclined to record it, and those instances where it does appear in surviving\
  \ records are limited and opaque. This article aims to draw attention to several\
  \ distinct sets of sources that reference churching. These fragments offer glimpses\
  \ of how churching ceremonies (and the early days of maternity) were understood\
  \ by communities and families and experienced by women in the throes of \u2018matrescence\u2019\
  ."

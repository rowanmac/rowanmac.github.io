abstract: "Ireland has an ancient tradition of hospitality. Scholarship on the role\
  \ of hospitality within funerary traditions in Ireland notes the dearth of descriptions\
  \ of the particular foods consumed. Little research exists on this topic concerning\
  \ the twenty-first century. Using a combination of autoethnography, folklore, and\
  \ historical and literary sources, this article reviews the field and argues for\
  \ the consoling role that food and drink play in Irish death traditions. A link\
  \ between rising wealth during the Celtic Tiger period (1995\u20132007) and sit-down\
  \ meals in hotels is identified. The autoethnography focuses on funerary customs\
  \ in the Connemara Gaeltacht over the last four decades, but also draws on memories\
  \ and stories of funerals in large cities, rural towns, and throughout the four\
  \ provinces. This research is significant as it marries autoethnography with Irish-language\
  \ and anglophone literature, including recent creative writing, while also discussing\
  \ how COVID affected Irish funerary traditions."

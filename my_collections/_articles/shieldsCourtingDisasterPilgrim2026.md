abstract: "This article addresses an important lacuna in scholarship by examining\
  \ mobility in late medieval Ireland through pilgrims travelling to Saint Patrick\u2019\
  s Purgatory, c.1350\u2013c.1550. Using first-hand narratives alongside administrative\
  \ and ecclesiastical records, it challenges the view that travel through Gaelic\
  \ Ireland was inherently perilous, arguing instead that pilgrims could generally\
  \ move across both English and Gaelic territories with relative ease. Focusing on\
  \ four continental travellers\u2014Ramon de Perell\xF3s, Laurence Rathold, Ghillebert\
  \ de Lannoy and Francesco Chiericati\u2014it explores how political authority, social\
  \ status and infrastructure shaped movement. These accounts highlight the role of\
  \ safe-conducts, hospitality, translation and aristocratic patronage in facilitating\
  \ travel, while risks were mitigated by networks of elite protection. Although elite\
  \ pilgrims arguably benefited most, evidence also points to wider participation.\
  \ Ultimately, this article reframes late medieval Ireland as an interconnected landscape\
  \ where pilgrimage enabled cross-cultural interaction."

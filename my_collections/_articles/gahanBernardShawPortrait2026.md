abstract: "This biographical article traces young adult Bernard Shaw\u2019s\ninteractions\
  \ with Irish\u2014as well as some significant\nnon-Irish\u2014people during his\
  \ first twenty-two years living in London\nin order to give an idea of what being\
  \ Irish meant in the Imperial\ncapital at the end of the nineteenth century. This\
  \ first part\ncovers the eighteen years from 1876 to just before his first London\n\
  theatrical success. The second part (to be published in a future\nissue of \nSHAW)\
  \ will begin with that 1894 Avenue Theatre Season and\nconclude four years later\
  \ in May and June 1898, when the\nforty-one-year-old Irish socialist\u2019s life\
  \ took a completely new\nturn with the publication of his first two volumes of plays\
  \ (\nPlays Pleasant and Unpleasant), his becoming a full-time\ndramatist on retiring\
  \ as a full-time critic, and his marriage to\nIrishwoman Charlotte Payne-Townshend.\
  \ Taken together, these Irish\nconnections of the early London years laid the foundations\
  \ for\nShaw\u2019s considerable involvement in Irish affairs, cultural and\npolitical,\
  \ in the first quarter of the twentieth century."

abstract: This article provides a diplomatic analysis of the eight surviving letters
  sent by Irish kings to the kings of England in the thirteenth century, while an
  edition and translation of these letters is given in the Appendix. Using this corpus,
  this article puts forward three arguments. First, the Irish kings were deeply familiar
  with the sophisticated European culture of letter writing, thus refuting past claims
  of these kings being warmongering barbarians on the periphery of Christendom. Second,
  the medium of letter-writing was central in the efforts of Irish kings to perpetuate
  their status and political position, framing themselves as participants in the political
  culture rather than opposed to it. Third, the Irish kings demonstrated their perspicacity
  through their letters by highlighting their knowledge of broader archipelagic events,
  thus promoting the idea of political harmony that was stressed in contemporary letter-writing
  manuals.

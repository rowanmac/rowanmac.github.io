abstract: "This article argues for a re-appraisal of Anglo-Irish relations in the\
  \ mid-twentieth century. We challenge dominant narratives within the historiography\
  \ which have foregrounded political division, disruption and violence. Our counter\
  \ narrative is achieved by interrogating the history of Anglo-Irish relations through\
  \ the lens of non-combatant and non-party political women\u2019s activism. Consequently,\
  \ the article reveals an overlooked history of solidarity, friendship and cross-borders\
  \ co-operation via the case-studies of five popular voluntary women\u2019s organisations.\
  \ These are: the Mothers\u2019 Union; the Girls\u2019 Friendly Society; the Irish\
  \ Countrywomen\u2019s Association; the Federation of Women\u2019s Institutes of\
  \ Northern Ireland and the National Federation of Women\u2019s Institutes. By tracing\
  \ the everyday \u2018quiet\u2019 activism of these grassroots women\u2019s organisations\
  \ from the 1920s to the 1970s, we evidence how they nurtured dynamic networks of\
  \ solidarity across the island of Ireland, and in Britain. Their endeavours were\
  \ sustained over time by a shared desire to enhance the lives of women and girls.\
  \ Our analysis offers fresh insights and opens up new debates about multiple expressions\
  \ of female activism. In doing so we re-shape understandings of women\u2019s grassroots\
  \ activism and highlight its impact on twentieth century Anglo-Irish history."

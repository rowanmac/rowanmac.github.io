abstract: "This article explores the difficult role played by Christopher Wandesford\
  \ (1592\u20131640) when he acted as deputy to Sir Thomas Wentworth (later earl of\
  \ Strafford), lord deputy and then lord lieutenant of Ireland from 1632\u201340.\
  \ Wentworth was reliant on a small group of close associates whom he could trust\
  \ to act on his behalf during time away from Ireland. Much of their work was carried\
  \ out behind the scenes, meaning it has received limited attention from historians\
  \ who have focused on Wentworth himself. This article foregrounds for the first\
  \ time the importance of Christopher Wandesford, who acted as a lord justice during\
  \ the 1630s, and then lord deputy of Ireland in 1640. This was an unenviable basis\
  \ on which to represent the king\u2019s interests, since being the \u2018deputy\u2019\
  s deputy\u2019 did not afford him the authority to keep the Irish parliament in\
  \ check. Wandesford tried, and failed, to control the Irish parliament in Wentworth\u2019\
  s absence. This article demonstrates the difficulties of governing Ireland from\
  \ afar, especially when Wentworth was absent, and the limits of the role of the\
  \ king\u2019s representative\u2019s representative."

abstract: "This special issue examines women\u2019s grassroots activism in Britain\
  \ and Ireland from 1918 to the late twentieth century. Emerging from the AHRC-funded\
  \ network \u2018Agency and Advocacy: Locating Women\u2019s Grassroots Activism in\
  \ England and Ireland, 1918 to the Present\u2019, it brings together eleven articles\
  \ that explore how women organised within voluntary associations, community groups,\
  \ workplaces, and social movements. The collection challenges protest-centred definitions\
  \ of activism by highlighting a broader range of practices, including education\
  \ and information sharing, community service, and craftivism. In particular, this\
  \ Introduction and the collection more widely argue for a greater emphasis to be\
  \ placed on \u2018quiet\u2019 forms of activism. Contributors demonstrate how everyday\
  \ experiences shaped women\u2019s pathways into collective action and political\
  \ engagement. The articles reveal the importance of local contexts while also tracing\
  \ connections that transcended national or organisational boundaries. Collectively,\
  \ the articles argue for a more inclusive understanding of activism and activist\
  \ identity, demonstrating that women\u2019s grassroots organising has played a vital\
  \ role in improving the lives of women and girls across twentieth-century Britain\
  \ and Ireland."

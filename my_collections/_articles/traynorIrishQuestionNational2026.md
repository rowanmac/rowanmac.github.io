abstract: "The outbreak of the Troubles represented a crisis and opportunity for the\
  \ fledgling National Front. Unlike other British parties, the \u201CIrish Question\u201D\
  \ remained an important ideological preoccupation of the National Front. It conspiratorially\
  \ saw the conflict as a deliberate attempt by international forces to undermine\
  \ the British nation. This unscored the party\u2019s advocacy of staunch British\
  \ loyalism, which influenced its propaganda output and saw the party organise pro-loyalist\
  \ demonstrations in English cities. But these activities failed to capture the same\
  \ levels of support among the public as its anti-immigration campaigning had. Meanwhile,\
  \ the National Front\u2019s entanglement with Ulster loyalism in Northern Ireland\
  \ proved unfruitful. Their respective visions of loyalism proved irreconcilable,\
  \ illuminating the fundamentally different worldviews of the British far right and\
  \ Ulster loyalists."

abstract: "Plastic Paddy, an epithet of derision and subjugation emerging during the\
  \ 1980s/1990s, attained an international iconic representational status. In response,\
  \ the paper suggests a critical intervention, A Critical Sociology of Diaspora Epistemology,\
  \ enabling a re-reading of the complexity of socio-cultural relations between 1980s/1990s\
  \ elite migrants and second-generation diaspora in Britain mediated by Irish state\
  \ governance, regulation and bordering. A wider context is the complexities of displaced\
  \ Irish anxieties\u2019 captured in a state-mediated generational class-based internal\
  \ nationalism that continues to be played out. The Republic\u2019s post-Independence\
  \ national(ist) stance of demographic and symbolic bordering/re-bordering \u2013\
  \ \u201Cwe can\u2019t all live on a small island\u201D \u2013 resonates across public\
  \ life. This includes: the 1950s generation \u201Cas not real emigrants,\u201D the\
  \ second generation as \u201Cnot real Irish\u201D and the 2004 Referendum removal\
  \ of automatic entitlement to citizenship for Irish-born children as \u201Cnot real\
  \ nationals,\u201D alongside populism\u2019s (early 2020s) far-right slogans: \u201C\
  Ireland is full.\u201D"

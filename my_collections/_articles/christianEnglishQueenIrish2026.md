abstract: "This article examines depictions of Elizabeth I in two\nIrish-authored\
  \ plays from the early twentieth century, Lady\nGregory\u2019s \nThe Canavansand\
  \ Shaw\u2019s \nThe Dark Lady of the Sonnets. Both dramatists had, in\nearlier plays,\
  \ used women as symbols to dramatize the plight of\ncolonized Ireland and the struggle\
  \ for independence. Their\nportrayals of Elizabeth, showing her as person, myth,\
  \ and English\nicon, offered a more indirect commentary on Irish-English\nrelations.\
  \ Both plays borrowed from a variety of images of the\nQueen circulated in Ireland\
  \ and England from the fifteenth century\nto the twentieth, ranging from grotesque\
  \ monster to fairy queen.\nThe dramatists use humor and irony to hold these worshipful\
  \ and\ndemonizing images at arm\u2019s length, suggesting that Elizabeth\u2019s\n\
  symbolic personae are tools that can be used by shrewd\ncommunicators not only to\
  \ buttress English authority, but also to\nsubvert it."

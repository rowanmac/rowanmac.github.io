abstract: "Focussing on the period from 1970 to 1990, this article examines how gay\
  \ men, many of whom moved from Ireland to London, adapted their activism in the\
  \ face of evolving housing pressures. It pays particular attention to how housing\
  \ reforms, the AIDS crisis and notions of national identity shaped gay politics\
  \ during this period and how the social crises of the 1980s transformed gay radicalism\
  \ as conflict over identities and AIDS redefined gay activism and interactions with\
  \ the state. I bring together two traditions in a way that has not been extensively\
  \ done by historians. The historiography of the Irish in Britain has done little\
  \ to look at homosexuality, while the historiography of gay Britain rarely considers\
  \ the issue of post-war immigration, still less Irish migration. I put into conversation\
  \ these historiographies which have hitherto often passed as ships in the night.\
  \ The article locates the responses of Irish gay activists to the turmoil of the\
  \ long 1980s within broader histories of radical politics in this period and national\
  \ transformations of housing provision. In doing so, the article suggests that gay\
  \ men\u2019s housing activism was itself indicative of wider coalitional transformations\
  \ in gay politics."
